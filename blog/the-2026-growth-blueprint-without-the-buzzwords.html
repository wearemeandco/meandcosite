The 2026 Growth Blueprint (Without the Buzzwords)

January 28, 2026

Hola familia, let’s be honest for a second.

We have more tools than ever. More platforms. More “AI this” and “automation that.” And yet… most founders feel more tired, more behind, and more overwhelmed than they did five years ago.

That’s not a personal failure. That’s the environment. Here’s what I’m seeing as we head into 2026, especially for small businesses, studios, and founder-focused organizations, and what actually matters when you strip away the hype.

1. Faster Doesn’t Mean Better (Sometimes It Just Means Louder)

Here’s the weird thing about modern tools: they’re supposed to make things easier… but often they just add weight.

New tech goes from “brand new” to “everyone’s using it” in what feels like five minutes. By the time you’ve watched a webinar about it, the moment has already passed. So the goal can’t be “learn every new tool.”

The real shift is this: stop stacking tools. Start fixing systems. Let’s say this again, stop stacking tools AND START FIXING SYSTEMS.

If your process is messy, automating it just makes the mess faster. Trust me. The founders who win aren’t tool collectors. They’re people who design simpler ways to work, decide faster, and remove friction for themselves and their teams. Speed comes from clarity, not more software.

2. AI Isn’t a Tool Anymore. It’s More Like a Team Member.

We’ve moved past “AI that helps you write a caption or an email.”

Now we’re entering the era of AI that does things, handles steps, makes decisions, follows rules, checks work, and hands you something usable.

That sounds exciting. It is. It’s also where a lot of people mess up.

The biggest mistake I am seeing? People automate broken workflows. So they are taking any new tool and trying to add it to their stack.

If your intake is unclear, your messaging is fuzzy, or your approvals are chaotic, AI won’t save you. It’ll just amplify the confusion.

The smart move in 2026 is simple: redesign first. Automate second.

We all know that if AI is set up well, it can handle repetitive tasks, track what’s working, and support growth without burning people out. But it still needs human direction. This is exactly what Ivette and I were speaking to on Tuesday in her podcast, BizTalks (stay tuned for our episode — coming out in February 2026 and subscribe to their channel here). We are still the creative, the visionaries and the leaders in design!

3. Search Isn’t About Google Anymore. It’s About Being the Answer.

People don’t “search” the way they used to.

They ask questions. They expect direct answers. And increasingly, those answers come from AI summaries, not endless links from Google search.

Here’s the uncomfortable truth: If your content is generic, it disappears.

In 2026, visibility comes from saying something specific and real:

  • Your lived experience (testimonies, learnings, failures, etc).
  • Your numbers (how you have grown, changed, evolved, etc).
  • Your point of view (thought leadership babbyy!)
  • Your founder perspective (subject matter expertise).

The brands that win aren’t louder. They’re clearer.

And clarity doesn’t come from posting more—it comes from knowing what to say and why. 🎤

4. Polished Is Out. Real Is the Advantage.

Perfect branding is no longer impressive. Behind-the-scenes beats highlight reels — and thank God because this is my favorite place to be. Founder stories outperform corporate posts. Messy honesty builds more trust than glossy campaigns.

Why? Because people are drowning in AI-generated sameness. Check out our stories from time to time, we post “Is this real?” content to see how well you can distinguish AI from REAL life.

Here’s an idea and an example of what cuts through the noise. A founder explaining why they made a decision, what didn’t work, and what they learned along the way.

This is why founder-led content consistently outperforms brand accounts. People don’t want a company talking at them. They want a human talking with them. You know this is true. How many times do you dial “0” when seeking to speak to an operator? (If I am honest I dial — 0000000000). Ha.

In a world full of automation, being human is the moat.

5. The Old Agency Model Is Fading (And Honestly… Good, because we never liked that word).

Deliverables alone aren’t enough anymore. Clients don’t just want a website or a content calendar. They want momentum. Revenue. Direction.

That’s why studios (mine included) are shifting away from one-off assets and toward embedded strategy, fractional leadership, and long-term growth support. Less checking boxes. More owning outcomes.

Marketing is becoming a managed function—not a pile of tasks.

6. People Want Two Things at Once: Depth and Entertainment

Consumption habits look contradictory, but they actually make a lot of sense. We’ve all seen those posts where someone shows how to make a quick, healthy snack in 30 seconds. No intro. No backstory. Just: here’s the problem, here’s the solution, moving on. I have saved these lately and ACTUALLY been doing them. My favorite? The banana & coconut flakes cookie recipe — super simple!

That kind of content works because it respects people’s time.

At the same time, those same people will happily spend an hour listening to a podcast, watching a long interview, or following a founder’s journey over time. This isn’t confusion—it’s context switching.

Short-form content satisfies the moment: “I need something quick, useful, or entertaining right now.”

Long-form content builds trust: “I want to understand how this person thinks.”

The mistake brands make is choosing one or the other.

In 2026, the most effective strategy is BOTH!

  • Depth for authority (long-form conversations, podcasts, thoughtful posts)
  • Short-form for visibility (quick tips, behind-the-scenes moments, simple demos)

One strong idea can—and should—do both jobs.

When you start with depth, short-form becomes easier. You’re not scrambling for ideas; you’re just pulling moments from thinking you’ve already done.

Ok lean in: AI can help with distribution, repurposing, and speed. But humans still stay for story, perspective, and meaning.

That’s the balance founders need to build for now. That’s it. Simple.

Final Thought: The Goal Isn’t “Using AI”

The goal for 2026 isn’t to keep up with every trend.

It’s to build a business that:

  • Feels lighter to run.
  • Uses tech to reduce friction (and we are actively working on that everyday, so you are not alone).
  • Centers human judgment and connection (human first, always).

If all the repetitive work in your business disappeared tomorrow… what’s the one meaningful problem you’d finally have space to solve?

That’s where growth actually lives.
